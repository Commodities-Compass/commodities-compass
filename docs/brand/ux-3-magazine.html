I Market Analysis

Cocoa futures surged to fresh highs this week as the market digested another round of disappointing crop data from West Africa. The ICE New York front-month contract closed at $8,247 per metric tonne, extending its year-to-date advance to 34 percent and marking the highest weekly close since the historic spike in April 2024. London futures followed suit, with the July contract settling at £6,512, up 2.8 percent on the week.

The rally is underpinned by an increasingly dire supply picture. The International Cocoa Organization revised its 2025/26 global deficit estimate to 374,000 tonnes, the third consecutive year of shortfall and the largest since the 2015/16 El Niño event. Côte d'Ivoire, which accounts for roughly 40 percent of global production, has seen cumulative port arrivals lag last season by 12 percent through early May, with mid-crop expectations being downgraded across the board.

Supply Dynamics

Ghana's Cocobod has announced a further cut to its 2025/26 production target, now standing at 620,000 tonnes — well below the 750,000-tonne ambition articulated just two seasons ago. The combination of aging tree stock, swollen shoot virus disease, and erratic rainfall patterns has created structural headwinds that cannot be resolved within a single crop cycle.

Meanwhile, grindings data continues to signal demand destruction at the margins. European Q1 grindings fell 4.2 percent year-on-year, while North American processors reported a 3.1 percent decline. However, Asian demand remains resilient with a 1.8 percent increase, suggesting the premium chocolate segment and emerging market appetite are partially offsetting weakness in traditional consuming regions.

Technical Outlook

From a technical perspective, the breakout above $8,000 resistance is significant. The 20-day moving average has crossed above the 50-day, generating a golden cross signal that historically precedes extended moves in cocoa. Open interest has expanded by 8 percent over the past two weeks, suggesting new money is entering the market rather than short-covering driving the advance.

The Commitment of Traders report shows managed money net-long positioning at 42,000 contracts, elevated but not yet at the extreme levels seen during the March 2024 peak of 58,000 contracts. This suggests the speculative community has room to add further length. The term structure remains in steep backwardation, with the July/September spread at $380 per tonne, reinforcing the tightness narrative and discouraging producer selling.

The cocoa market is experiencing the most severe structural supply deficit in a generation. Three consecutive years of shortfall have depleted buffer stocks to critical levels.

X Forward Outlook
Short-Term (1-4 Weeks)

The immediate outlook favours further upside testing. The breakout above $8,000 has established a new base, and momentum indicators remain constructive despite the elevated RSI reading of 68.4. Key catalysts in the near term include the May 16 CFTC CoT report, which is expected to show continued managed money accumulation, and the publication of April port arrivals data from Côte d'Ivoire.

Risk of pullback exists around the $8,600 resistance level, which corresponds to the 61.8% Fibonacci retracement of the March 2024 to January 2025 decline. However, any correction to the $7,800 support zone should be viewed as a buying opportunity within the current OPEN signal framework.

Medium-Term (1-3 Months)

The medium-term trajectory depends critically on mid-crop arrivals and the June/July weather patterns across West Africa. Current meteorological models suggest below-average rainfall probability of 60 percent for the Sahel belt through July, which would further compromise yields and extend the supply deficit.

Grindings data will provide the key demand-side variable. If Q2 grindings show further decline beyond 5 percent year-on-year, pricing power may moderate as the market begins to discount accelerated substitution and reformulation by confectioners. Compass will recalibrate the demand component of its composite score accordingly.

Strategic View (3-12 Months)

Structurally, the cocoa market faces multi-year supply challenges that are unlikely to be resolved before the 2027/28 crop at the earliest. Tree replanting programmes in Ghana require 3-5 years to reach productive maturity, and the swollen shoot virus continues to spread at an alarming rate. These biological constraints set a floor under prices that is unprecedented in modern market history.

The Compass composite score of 72 reflects this structural view. While tactical volatility is expected and short-term corrections are inevitable, the directional bias remains firmly bullish. The OPEN signal will be maintained unless the composite score falls below 55, which would require a material improvement in supply fundamentals or a sharp deterioration in speculative positioning.
